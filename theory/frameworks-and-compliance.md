# Security Frameworks, Controls & Compliance

## 1. Core Concepts
- **Security Lifecycle:** A constantly evolving set of policies and standards that define how an organization manages risks, follows guidelines, and meets regulatory compliance.
- **Security Frameworks:** Guidelines used for building plans to help mitigate risks and threats to data and privacy. They provide a structured approach to implementing a security lifecycle.
- **Security Controls:** Safeguards designed to reduce specific security risks (e.g., requiring employees to complete privacy training).
- **Compliance:** The process of adhering to internal standards and external regulations (laws) to avoid fines and breaches.
- **CIA Triad:** Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability. The three foundational principles used to establish controls that mitigate threats, risks, and vulnerabilities.

## 2. The 4 Core Components of a Security Framework
1. **Identifying and documenting security goals:** (e.g., aligning with GDPR).
2. **Setting guidelines to achieve security goals:** (e.g., developing policies for handling data requests).
3. **Implementing strong security processes:** (e.g., designing procedures to comply with verified user data requests).
4. **Monitoring and communicating results:** (e.g., monitoring the network and reporting potential issues to management).

## 3. Key Frameworks & Regulations (Must Know for Interviews)

### NIST (National Institute of Standards and Technology)
A U.S.-based agency that develops voluntary compliance frameworks used worldwide.
- **NIST CSF (Cybersecurity Framework)**
- **NIST RMF (Risk Management Framework)**

### FERC-NERC (Federal Energy Regulatory Commission - North American Electric Reliability Corporation)
- **Applies to:** Organizations working with electricity or the U.S./North American power grid.
- **Requirement:** Obligated to prepare for, mitigate, and report security incidents affecting the power grid. Must adhere to Critical Infrastructure Protection (CIP) standards.

### FedRAMP (Federal Risk and Authorization Management Program)
- **Applies to:** U.S. federal government and third-party cloud providers.
- **Purpose:** Standardizes security assessment, authorization, monitoring, and handling of cloud services for consistency.

### CIS (Center for Internet Security)
- **Applies to:** All organizations.
- **Purpose:** Provides a set of actionable controls to safeguard systems and networks against attacks. Helps establish a better plan of defense.

### GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)
- **Applies to:** E.U. residents' data (even outside E.U. territory).
- **Purpose:** Protects the processing of E.U. residents' data and their right to privacy.
- **Key Rule:** If a breach occurs, the affected organization has **72 hours** to notify the E.U. citizen.

### PCI DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard)
- **Applies to:** Organizations that store, accept, process, or transmit credit card information.
- **Purpose:** Ensures a secure environment to reduce credit card fraud.

### HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act)
- **Applies to:** U.S. federal law protecting patients' health information (PHI).
- **Rules:** Privacy, Security, Breach Notification.
- **Note:** Governed alongside HITRUST (Health Information Trust Alliance), a framework to help meet HIPAA compliance.

### ISO (International Organization for Standardization)
- **Applies to:** Global organizations.
- **Purpose:** Establishes international standards for technology, manufacturing, and management to improve processes and procedures.

### SOC 1 & SOC 2 (System and Organizations Controls)
- **Created by:** AICPA (American Institute of Certified Public Accountants).
- **Purpose:** Reports that focus on an organization's user access policies at different levels (Associate, Supervisor, Manager, Executive, Vendor) to assess financial compliance and risk levels.
- **Covers:** Confidentiality, privacy, integrity, availability, security, and data safety.

## 4. Pro Tip for Interviews
Mention that you understand the difference between **Frameworks** (guidelines/plans) and **Controls** (specific safeguards). Also, be aware that regulations are frequently revised. Keep an eye on the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act and the Sarbanes-Oxley Act for future study.
