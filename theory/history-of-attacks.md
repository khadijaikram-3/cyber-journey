# The History of Cyber Attacks & Malware

Understanding past attacks helps security analysts predict and handle future incidents. Attackers often alter or enhance previous methods.

## 1. The Evolution of Attacks
### The Brain Virus (1986)
- **Created by:** The Alvi brothers.
- **Intention:** To track illegal copies of medical software and prevent pirated licenses.
- **What actually happened:** The virus infected any computer that used the pirated software, and spread to any disk inserted into that computer. It spread globally within months.
- **Impact:** Slowed down productivity and significantly impacted business operations. It altered the computing industry, showing the need for security and productivity plans.

### The Morris Worm (1988)
- **Created by:** Robert Morris.
- **Intention:** A program to assess the size of the internet by crawling the web and installing itself on computers to tally the number of connected computers.
- **What actually happened:** The program failed to track already-compromised computers, so it kept reinstalling itself until computers ran out of memory and crashed.
- **Impact:** Affected ~6,000 computers (10% of the internet at the time). Caused millions in damages. 
- **Legacy:** Led to the creation of **CERTs (Computer Emergency Response Teams)** to respond to security incidents.

### The LoveLetter Attack (2000)
- **Created by:** Onel De Guzman.
- **Intention:** To steal internet login credentials.
- **How it worked:** Users received an email titled "I Love You" with an attachment. When opened, it scanned the user's address book and sent itself to everyone on the list. Recipients thought it was from a friend.
- **Impact:** Infected 45 million computers globally, caused over $10 billion in damages.
- **Legacy:** The **first example of Social Engineering**.

### The Equifax Breach (2017)
- **What happened:** Attackers infiltrated the credit reporting agency, Equifax.
- **Impact:** Stole over 143 million customer records (affecting 40% of Americans). Records included PII (social security numbers, birth dates, driver's licenses, addresses, credit card numbers).
- **Why it happened:** Multiple failures by Equifax to fix known vulnerabilities in the months leading up to the breach. 
- **Legacy:** Equifax paid over $575 million in settlements. It alerted companies to the massive financial impact of a breach and the need for preventative measures.

## 2. Malware Types
- **Virus:** Malicious code written to interfere with computer operations and cause damage. It requires a user to initiate it (e.g., by opening a malicious attachment). It hides in other files and spreads when those files are opened.
- **Worm:** Malware that can duplicate and spread itself across systems **on its own**. It does not need a user to download it; it self-replicates from an infected computer to other devices on the same network.
- **Ransomware:** A malicious attack where threat actors encrypt an organization's data and demand payment to restore access.
- **Spyware:** Malware used to gather and sell information without consent. It collects private emails, texts, voice/image recordings, and locations.

## 3. Social Engineering
**Social Engineering** is a manipulation technique that exploits human error to gain private information, access, or valuables. It relies on trusting someone without question.

### Common Types of Social Engineering
- **Social media phishing:** Collecting detailed information from social media to launch an attack.
- **Watering hole attack:** Attacking a website frequently visited by a specific group of users.
- **USB baiting:** Leaving a malware-infected USB stick for an employee to find and plug in.
- **Physical social engineering:** Impersonating an employee, customer, or vendor to gain unauthorized physical access.

### The 7 Principles of Social Engineering (Why it works)
1. **Authority:** Impersonating individuals with power (people are conditioned to respect authority).
2. **Intimidation:** Using bullying tactics to persuade/intimidate victims.
3. **Consensus/Social Proof:** Pretending that "everyone else is doing it" to gain trust.
4. **Scarcity:** Implying that goods or services are in limited supply.
5. **Familiarity:** Establishing a fake emotional connection.
6. **Trust:** Building an emotional relationship over time to gain information.
7. **Urgency:** Persuading people to respond quickly without questioning.
